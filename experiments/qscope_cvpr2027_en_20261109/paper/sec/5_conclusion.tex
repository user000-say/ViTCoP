\section{Conclusion}
We formulate visual-token pruning as query-adaptive subset selection under a layered budget and a coverage-fidelity constraint, and we reveal three coupled limitations that this formulation separates: salience changes with visual depth, visual coverage is not fully consistent with the question, and a capacity fixed before the language model cannot serve every interaction stage. Q-SCOPE builds a dual-depth regional basis, edits it with query-guided exchange, and contracts the nested set with question-to-visual attention. On the completed LLaVA-1.5-7B schedule that removes 94.4\% of the visual token-layer budget, the six-task relative mean is 95.66\%. TextVQA drops while POPE and GQA rise. The remaining budgets, transfer models, video benchmarks, and latency measurements are left blank until they are run under the same protocol.
